Closed loop: keep the cursor on the target

Every 50 ms the encoder stimulates the left or right sensory electrodes () on the side the target is on, more often the further away it is. The decoder moves the cursor by the difference in spikes between the right and left halves of the array.

zero sends no stimulation. random_matched sends the same amount, on a random side. real should win, but only because a stimulus excites the side it lands on: this is a reflex arc, not learning.

What is being simulated
  • 1,024 Izhikevich neurons, 80% regular-spiking excitatory and 20% fast-spiking inhibitory, scattered over a dish about 2 mm across. The model is integrated in two 0.5 ms half-steps per 1 ms tick.
  • 81,920 synapses, 80 per neuron, chosen with probability exp(−distance / 0.24 mm), with conduction delays of 1–8 ms.
  • Short-term depression: each spike uses 25% of a neuron's synaptic resources, which recover over 1.5 s. Depletion helps end each network burst and spaces them out, giving the signature pattern of cultured networks (switch it off to see the difference).
  • STDP on excitatory-to-excitatory synapses, with exponential traces (τ = 20 ms) and bounded weights.
  • 64 electrodes on an 8×8 grid. Each records neurons within ~64 µm and stimulates neurons within ~150 µm. The scope trace is synthetic: a spike waveform per recorded spike, plus noise and a stimulation artifact.

A toy built to look and behave like a cultured network at a glance. It is not fitted to any culture, organoid or hardware, and nothing here is evidence about biological neurons.
